# UK Accounting SMEs: AI Adoption and Operational Readiness, 2026

Status: complete and approved for publication  
Prepared: 2026-08-01  
Owner: research director / product owner  
Research mode: secondary data only

## Objective

Produce one denominator-safe current-state report that combines the five general-report dimensions for UK accounting SMEs: AI use, integration, governance, use cases and adoption pathways.

The report is titled **UK Accounting SMEs: AI Adoption and Operational Readiness, 2026**. “Readiness” is an organising label, not a composite score.

## Scope

- UK enterprises whose principal activity is SIC 2007 class 69.20: accounting, bookkeeping and auditing activities; tax consultancy.
- SMEs with 0-249 employees, including non-employing practices where the source frame supports them.
- Accounting practices are in scope; in-house finance teams in other industries are out of scope.
- The study uses public or free-to-read secondary data only. No survey, interview or other primary data collection is permitted.
- Benefits, effectiveness, ROI, vendor comparison and client-business outcomes are reserved for a later research programme.

## Research question

What does the best available open secondary evidence show about the current extent, type and operational depth of AI adoption among UK accounting SMEs?

## Evidence decision

No identified official open dataset directly estimates the share of UK SIC 69.20 SMEs using AI. The final report therefore triangulates three evidence layers without combining their percentages:

1. Direct accounting-practice surveys for adoption, tools, tasks, barriers and preparedness.
2. Official broad SIC M evidence as contextual proxy only.
3. ONS SIC 69.20 business demography as the direct population frame.

This is sufficient for a bounded current-state assessment, but insufficient for one exact population prevalence estimate. The evidence gap is disclosed and remains unresolved; primary research is not proposed because the research director has restricted the programme to secondary data.

## Publication conclusions

- AI is no longer niche among accounting practices, but measured adoption is definition-sensitive.
- The strongest direct benchmark reports 26% adopted and 54% piloting or adopted in April 2024.
- A later self-selected AccountingWEB sample reports 71.38% using external non-embedded tools in 2026. It is not a population estimate and is not a trend comparison with 2024.
- Current use is predominantly assistive and task-level: tax and information research, drafting, summarisation, client chatbots, document processing and reporting. Automation and forecasting are also visible.
- External tools and vendor-embedded features are more prominent than bespoke development or automated decision-making.
- Security, skills, integration and human oversight are separate operational capabilities and cannot be inferred from use alone.

## Denominator and comparison controls

- Keep all-business, AI-user, practice-level and individual-response denominators separate.
- Do not add multiple-response use-case percentages.
- Keep piloting, external-tool use, embedded AI, workflow integration and transformation as different concepts.
- Display fieldwork and publication dates separately.
- Retain confidence intervals and rounded bases where supplied.
- Label broad SIC M figures as contextual proxies.
- Do not create an AI-readiness score or infer impact.
