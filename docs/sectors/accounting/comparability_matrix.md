# Accounting AI evidence comparability matrix

Prepared: 2026-08-01  
Status: final

## Grades

- **A - direct frame:** matches the target sector and population concept.
- **B - direct directional:** accounting-specific, but the design does not support a representative SME population estimate.
- **C - contextual proxy:** official evidence for a broader sector, business-size scope or economy-wide population.
- **D - prohibited:** comparison would mix definitions or support an inference the source cannot justify.

| Proposed use | Source | Grade | Decision and control |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Count UK registered accounting SMEs | ONS UK business activity, Table 4, SIC 69.20 | A | Publish by employment band; disclose VAT/PAYE frame and base-5 rounding |
| Benchmark direct accounting-practice adoption | Sage/Demos/ACCA 2024 | B | Publish 26% adopted and 54% piloting/adopted with fieldwork, weighting and coverage limits |
| Describe current external-tool use | AccountingWEB/Sage 2026 | B | Publish as self-selected profession evidence; do not generalise to all accounting SMEs |
| Describe accounting tasks and technologies | Both accounting surveys | B | Keep source-defined lists separate; do not rank across surveys |
| Compare adoption, integration, policy and pathways in SIC M | UKBDS 2026 | C | Label every figure “broad SIC M contextual proxy”; retain denominators and CIs |
| Use official technology-type context | DSIT AI Adoption Research 2026 | C | Label economy-wide/broad-sector coverage and exclusion of firms below five employees |
| Treat FRC/ICAEW guidance as prevalence | FRC/ICAEW | D | Guidance may define responsible controls but never their prevalence |
| Average 26%, 54%, 71.38% and 50.6% | Mixed | D | Prohibited: different questions, samples, dates and denominators |
| Present 2024 and 2026 figures as a trend | Mixed | D | Prohibited: no consistent instrument or sampling design |
| Infer ROI, productivity or accuracy | Mixed | D | Prohibited in the current-state study |
| Create a readiness score | Mixed | D | Prohibited: no justified construct, variables or weights |

## Evidence-gap decision

The secondary evidence supports a directional conclusion about the breadth and type of use. It does not support one exact official prevalence estimate for UK accounting SMEs. The gap is disclosed rather than filled with a modelled estimate or original fieldwork.
