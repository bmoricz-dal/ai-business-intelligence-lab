# UK micro accounting practice: evidence-informed AI adoption case study

Status: complete and owner-approved for publication  
Prepared: 2026-08-03  
Research mode: secondary data only  
Publication status: approved and published on 2026-08-03

## Objective

Connect the accounting current-state and benefits studies to the general adoption-pathway framework through a detailed, reusable worked case for a UK micro accounting practice with 1–9 employees.

## Research question

How can a resource-constrained UK micro accounting practice select, test, control, measure and decide whether to scale different forms of AI adoption without treating prevalence, vendor claims or international effect estimates as guaranteed benefits?

## Case-study status

This is an evidence-informed composite scenario, not an empirical case study of an observed firm. The named practice, staffing, clients, systems, workloads, pilot sizes and decision thresholds are illustrative assumptions created to demonstrate the method. They must not be described as representative of UK accounting firms.

## Case definition

- Illustrative firm: Cedar Ledger Ltd, a fictional UK accounting practice.
- Size: seven payroll employees, within the official 1–9 employee micro-employer band and the 5–9 band covered by DSIT's 2025 survey.
- Illustrative roles: owner-director, senior accountant, three accountants/bookkeepers, payroll specialist and practice administrator.
- Illustrative operating environment: cloud accounting software, payroll software, document portal and email; no proprietary model or data-science team.
- Illustrative client mix: 180 small-business and sole-trader clients. This is a scenario input, not a sector estimate.
- Initial use case: transaction categorisation, reconciliation and month-end close support, because this has the strongest accounting-specific outcome evidence.

## Adoption methods

1. **Use** — an approved standalone assistant for internal research and first drafts, using non-client or properly controlled information.
2. **Integrate** — AI embedded in an existing accounting platform for transaction coding, reconciliation and exception handling.
3. **Automate** — bounded workflow steps such as exception routing and draft reminders, with approval before external action or ledger posting.
4. **Configure** — retrieval over approved internal procedures and templates; the micro practice configures a service rather than training a foundation model.
5. **Build** — assessed but normally rejected for this case because the capability, cost and assurance burden are disproportionate. It remains a decision outcome, not a mandatory maturity stage.

Governance is cross-cutting across every method. The pathways are choices, not a linear maturity score.

## Evidence hierarchy

| Grade | Evidence type | Role in the case |
|---|---|---|
| A | Experimental evidence for a defined accounting task | Supports a narrow task-effect statement only |
| B | Peer-reviewed operational or qualitative accounting evidence | Supports workflow mechanisms and adoption conditions |
| C | Official representative or weighted business evidence | Supports UK micro-business context and barriers |
| D | Authoritative guidance and professional workflow material | Supports controls, responsibilities and implementation actions |
| S | Peer-reviewed systematic or structured evidence synthesis | Supports cross-study adoption conditions; not an accounting effect size |

## Method

- Map every action to at least one evidence source or label it as an illustrative design choice.
- Keep observed research findings separate from the fictional firm's assumptions and from owner-set decision thresholds.
- Start with a four-week firm-specific baseline. Do not import productivity percentages as expected savings.
- Test on known or historical cases before live use, then run shadow mode before any controlled pilot.
- Include review, correction, training, assurance, licence and incident costs in the benefit boundary.
- Use pre-registered proceed, revise and stop rules; retain a manual fallback and rollback route.
- Prohibit autonomous tax conclusions, audit conclusions, material postings or external advice.

## Product hierarchy and deliverables

- **Primary output:** Accounting AI Experience Lab where visitors take one fictional client through six connected workstations: bookkeeping capture, ledger categorisation, reconciliation and close, management accounts, source-linked business insight, and final quality and audit-trail review.
- General Use, Integrate, Automate, Configure and Govern operating patterns are explained on the AI in Practice background page; method labels remain secondary annotations inside the accounting workstations.
- **Secondary output:** implementation planner where a firm can choose a pathway, complete steps, compare an aggregated baseline with a pilot, record gate decisions and export its browser session.
- Neither interactive workspace calls an AI model, connects to accounting software or transmits entered figures to DAL. Client-identifiable and confidential data are prohibited.
- Demonstration times, touches and control-coverage scores are deterministic scenario mechanics, not observed outcomes, UK accounting-SME benchmarks, product evaluations or forecasts.
- **Supporting output:** detailed PDF theoretical background, evidence boundaries and implementation method.
- Public step-level CSV that firms can copy into a project tracker.
- Evidence and source register with transfer and claim limits.
- Dedicated website route linked from both Accounting and Adoption pathways.
- Automated report, route and claim-boundary tests.
