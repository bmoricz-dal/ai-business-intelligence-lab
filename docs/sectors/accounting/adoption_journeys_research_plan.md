# Accounting AI Adoption Journeys, 2026 — research plan

## Objective

Use open secondary evidence to explain how accounting practices have moved from an operational problem to an AI-enabled workflow, including implementation choices, setbacks, adaptations, outcomes and changes to work. The study is a third accounting-sector output and complements, rather than replaces, the current-state and benefits reviews.

## Research question

What can open secondary sources show about why accounting firms adopted AI, how implementation proceeded, which setbacks or controls changed course, what outcomes were observed or reported, and what changed in work and services?

## Scope

- **Primary unit:** accounting practices and audit firms adopting AI in accounting or assurance work.
- **Primary geography:** UK where evidence exists; international evidence is retained with geography visible.
- **Firm size:** micro and SME evidence is prioritised. Large-firm evidence is included only where it illuminates an implementation mechanism unavailable in smaller-firm research.
- **Evidence type:** secondary data and published research only. No DAL survey, interview, observation or primary data collection.
- **Period:** sources available by 3 August 2026.
- **Practical link:** the findings explain why the Accounting AI Experience Lab uses bounded workflows, confidence cues, human review and staged implementation.

## Exclusions

- Vendor claims without a named workflow, implementation description or attributable client statement.
- General AI opinion pieces with no accounting implementation evidence.
- Unverifiable social-media anecdotes.
- Forecast benefits presented as realised outcomes.
- Finance-function examples presented as accounting-practice cases.
- Historical automation examples presented as AI adoption.
- A composite narrative that joins events from unrelated firms as though they happened to one firm.

## Evidence hierarchy

| Grade | Evidence role | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|
| A | Randomised or tightly framed experiment | Narrow causal effect inside the tested task and setting |
| B | Peer-reviewed operational or longitudinal evidence | Observed association, with selection and transfer limits |
| C | Peer-reviewed qualitative case or interview study | Implementation mechanism, experience and barrier—not prevalence or effect size |
| D | Regulator or professional-body aggregate/guidance | Sector context, risks and expected controls—not firm outcome |
| E | Named practice, provider-authored case or attributable practitioner self-report | Reported journey or outcome, visibly labelled and not independently verified |
| X | Historical non-AI automation comparator | Change-management lesson only; never evidence of AI impact |

Corroboration can strengthen confidence that a mechanism recurs. It does not upgrade a self-reported firm outcome into an independently measured result.

## Lifecycle schema

Every case is assessed against the same eleven fields:

1. Starting problem and operational context.
2. Adoption objective.
3. System or workflow selected.
4. Preparation and baseline.
5. Pilot or implementation sequence.
6. Staff and client response.
7. Setback, failure or constraint.
8. Adaptation, safeguard or control.
9. Outcome and outcome type.
10. Change to work, roles or services.
11. Transferability and unresolved gaps.

Missing fields remain marked **not reported**. They are not inferred.

## Case architecture

### Core accounting-practice evidence

1. **Integrated AI bookkeeping platform:** peer-reviewed US field evidence covering 79 private SME clients and a framed experiment with 99 accountants. This is the strongest outcome evidence, but the platform and firms are anonymised and adoption is endogenous outside the experiment.
2. **Love Your Accountants (LYA):** a named UK practice using Gemini, ChatGPT, Veryfi/Xero and Syft across selected tasks. Time savings are management estimates, not independently measured.
3. **Audit-firm implementation pattern:** a transparent synthesis of several peer-reviewed studies. It describes recurring mechanisms across firms; it is not represented as one named company journey.

### Historical change-management comparators

4. **Alfa Accountants/Beta:** a non-AI digital-transformation case showing a stalled internal initiative, a separate-team response, staged client onboarding and service-model change.
5. **Hudson Accountants:** a non-AI UK micro-practice automation journey showing incremental adoption, uneven tool economics and a move towards recurring bookkeeping and advisory work.

### Transfer cases, kept outside the core

Provider-authored accounts-payable, tax and finance-function examples from Deloitte, KPMG and PwC are used only to illustrate transferable implementation mechanics such as standardisation, confidence thresholds, human review and iterative retraining. They are not evidence about accounting-practice outcomes.

## Outcome rules

- **Experimental:** a difference attributable within a stated test design.
- **Associated:** observed alongside adoption but not established as caused by it.
- **Self-reported:** attributed to a named source and not independently verified.
- **Anticipated:** expected or planned; never written as achieved.
- **Comparator-only:** from non-AI automation or a non-practice finance function.
- **Not reported:** no outcome supplied by the source.

All numbers retain population, geography, period and evidence grade. No cross-case average, ROI estimate, vendor ranking or UK SME impact estimate is calculated.

## Synthesis method

The study uses a structured framework synthesis. Each source is registered, graded and mapped to lifecycle fields and outcome types. Cross-case findings require support from at least two independent source families or one high-quality field study plus contextual corroboration. Contradictions and missing evidence remain visible.

## Publication controls

- Page language must distinguish **observed**, **associated**, **self-reported** and **anticipated** outcomes.
- Large-firm, audit, international, provider-authored and historical comparator evidence must be labelled at point of use.
- Every public case row must trace to the source register.
- The research remains a secondary-evidence review, not implementation, accounting, tax, audit, legal or procurement advice.
- Local completion does not authorise GitHub publication or deployment. A separate research-director approval is required.

## Completion criteria

- Protocol, source register, evidence matrix, data dictionary and decision log are complete.
- The webpage renders case journeys, evidence grades, limitations and source links.
- Navigation and accounting-programme pages include the third sector study.
- Production tests and build pass.
- Publication status remains explicit until approval.
